The elevator is out again, so I take the stairs, counting floors by the smell. Two is curry. Three is bleach. Four is whatever Mrs. Okafor boils on Sundays, which she has explained to me twice and I have forgotten twice.

My door is open about an inch.

I stand on the landing and listen. The radiator knocks. Somebody's TV is playing a game show, the kind where people scream when they win a microwave. Inside my apartment, nothing. I push the door with two fingers.

The place has been tossed, but carefully, the way a cop would do it if the cop thought he might have to come back and pretend he'd never been there. Drawers are pulled out and put back crooked. The couch cushions are on the couch, just in the wrong order. My laptop is still on the table, which tells me more than anything else does.

I call Reyes.

"You're supposed to be off today," he says.

"Somebody was in my place."

"Was anything taken?"

"No."

"Then how do you know somebody was in your place?"

"Because the cushions are in the wrong order, Danny."

There's a pause, long enough that I can hear him deciding whether to make fun of me. He decides not to. "Don't touch anything," he says. "I'll send Kowalski."

"Kowalski couldn't find his own car."

"Then I'll send Kowalski with a map."

I hang up and sit on the arm of the couch, because the arm is the only part I'm sure nobody moved, and I look at the laptop. It is closed. I always leave it open.
